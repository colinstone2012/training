Module 5 - Anchoring, lines & knots

SBC Skipper · Module 5 of 8 · ≈ 55 minutes · Quiz: 10 questions served from a 30-question bank, 80% to pass

Anchoring badly is how good days end badly — a dragging hull into someone else's, a windlass injury, a lost anchor in a cable zone. This module teaches the SF545's system properly: a windlass with all-chain rode, a chain counter, and habits that make it boring.

Lesson 5.1 — Ground tackle: the SF545 system ≈ 6 min

»» CLUB MEDIA SLOTVIDEO - M5-02 windlass + chain counter
  • »»Windlass on the bow with an all-chain rode (the chain linking anchor to boat) — chain lays along the seabed and holds best. The rode end made fast aboard is the bitter end — check it's tied on before you need the anchor.
  • »»Chain counter at the helm — you always know how much chain is out.
  • »»Safety clip and strop (the strap that secures the anchor) — the anchor is clipped and stropped while stowed. Clip OFF to deploy; strop back on when stowed. An anchor that deploys itself while you're driving is not a theory.
  • »»Emergency manual release: the winch handle in the grab bag — fit it to the winch top and turn anti-clockwise to lower the anchor by hand, controlling the speed.
  • »»Windlass hazard: fingers, toes, hair and loose items well clear — always. It is the most dangerous machine on the boat.
»» KNOWLEDGE CHECKWhere does the emergency winch handle live? (In the grab bag, under the passenger seats.)

Lesson 5.2 — Choosing an anchorage ≈ 7 min

»» CLUB MEDIA SLOTVIDEO - M5-01 anchorage selection overhead

Five questions, in order:

  1. Shelter — the lee side of an island or headland (the sheltered side away from the wind) for today's wind (and tomorrow morning's shift).
  2. Depth — 5–6 metres is ideal. Never anchor deeper than 10 metres. Shallower than that in the wrong tide leaves you high and dry; deeper and your 5:1 chain gets long.
  3. Holding — sand and mud hold; weed, rock and shell don't.
  4. Swing room — room to swing in a 360° wind-or-tide change, clear of other boats (yours is not the only anchor dragging in a squall).
  5. Legality — no anchoring in channels or where you'd obstruct others; no anchoring or fishing in cable zones (charted — check your route); a Harbourmaster's approval is needed to anchor overnight in the same area beyond Auckland's 14-days-in-28 rule.

The plotter helps: bays suitable for anchoring usually carry an anchor symbol.

»» KNOWLEDGE CHECKPerfect bay, but the chart shows a cable symbol across the middle. What now? (No anchoring or fishing there — anchor outside the zone or move bays.)

Lesson 5.3 — Deploying the anchor ≈ 10 min

»» CLUB MEDIA SLOTVIDEO - M5-03 deploy + set

The procedure, start to finish:

  1. Approach at 5 kn ceiling — walking pace for the final 20 m — bow into the wind, and come to a complete stop. Use anchored boats as your wind indicator. Lower the anchor — never throw it. And never anchor from the stern: a stern-to-wind swing invites waves over the back of the boat.
  2. Release the safety clip on the chain.
  3. Deploy with the helm switch, letting chain run while giving gentle astern kicks — that lays the chain out along the bottom instead of piling it on the anchor. Watch the chain counter.
  4. Chain ratio: 5:1 is the club standard, in all conditions — for every metre of depth, 5 metres of chain. In very light conditions it may hold on less; more chain, never less. (Depth cap: 10 m maximum, 5–6 m ideal.)
  5. Set it: one more short kick astern, then take a transit — line up two fixed points ashore with part of your boat. If the points separate, you're still dragging: let out more chain and set again.
  6. Low light: navigation lights off, anchor light on.

If the windlass fails, the winch handle lowers the anchor by hand — that's why it's in the grab bag.

»» KNOWLEDGE CHECKDepth sounder reads 4 m, tide is dropping. How much chain? (5:1 → 20 m of chain, and remember the falling tide takes depth away from you — err on the generous side.)
»» KNOWLEDGE CHECKYour two transit points are slowly spreading apart after 10 minutes. Meaning? (You're dragging — let out more chain and re-set, or move.)

Lesson 5.4 — Retrieving the anchor ≈ 7 min

»» CLUB MEDIA SLOTVIDEO - M5-05 retrieve + trip reset
  1. Engine running before anything else.
  2. Two-person technique: one on the bow calling the chain angle and direction; one at the helm driving gently forward as the chain comes in. The hull drives toward the anchor — the windlass only lifts.
  3. Never winch alone: winching without driving overloads it and trips it out (loses power). If it trips: reset the trip switch — the yellow tag next to the battery switches.
  4. Chain bunching under the winch? Clear it as you go.
  5. Watch the anchor near the surface — a controlled ascent, not a swinging wreck toward your gelcoat (the hull's glossy outer layer).
  6. Reattach the safety strop before you drive off. Every time.

If it fouls: drive slowly over the anchor (engine in gear) to free it, and in shallow water it can be unsnagged by hand. A trip line on the anchor's crown (most anchors have a ring for it) makes fouling a non-event — and a small buoy on its end marks your anchor's position. Grounded on a falling tide? Kedging — row the anchor out to deeper water in the dinghy and winch the boat toward it — is the classic refloat.

»» KNOWLEDGE CHECKThe winch stops halfway up with a click. What happened and what now? (Winch tripped under load — drive the boat forward more, reset the yellow-tag trip switch by the battery switches, and continue with the hull doing the work.)

Lesson 5.5 — Anchoring law & etiquette ≈ 5 min

  • »»Never anchor in a channel, in a fairway, or so as to obstruct other vessels or the approach to a wharf.
  • »»Overnight in the same area: Auckland requires Harbourmaster approval beyond 14 days in any 28 (Appendix B).
  • »»Rafting up: ask before rafting alongside another boat, use your own fenders and lines, and agree who drops first and who leaves first.
  • »»Leave room: the boat that was there first owns the swing radius; arrive outside it.
  • »»Mooring buoys: only use moorings you have approval to use (private moorings belong to someone — $300 territory).
»» KNOWLEDGE CHECKA packed bay — one boat anchored dead centre with 30 m of chain out. Where do you anchor? (Outside their full swing circle — their chain counts as their radius, not just their hull.)

Lesson 5.6 — Moorings ≈ 4 min

Public or club-approved visitor moorings: approach into wind and current — 5 kn ceiling, walking pace for the final metres — pick up the pickup line with the boat hook from the bow, run the rope through a bow cleat (round turn and two half hitches — Lesson 5.7), adjust, done. Private moorings are laid and maintained with Harbourmaster approval for a specific vessel — using someone else's without approval is an offence, and they come up in court when they fail.

»» KNOWLEDGE CHECKA mooring buoy near your lunch spot looks sturdy and unattended. Can you use it? (Only if it's designated for visitor use — private moorings require the Harbourmaster's approval to use.)

Lesson 5.7 — Knots for this boat ≈ 8 min

»» CLUB MEDIA SLOTSTILLS - M5-06 knot close-ups

Seven knots cover everything the SF545 does — each with a job:

  1. Cleat hitch — the berthing and securing knot: around the cleat, two figure-eights, finish with a half hitch under the standing part. Holds hard, releases one-handed. (For the PB2 assessment and polished berthing; your everyday M1 dock connection is the bowline loop from Lesson 1.7.)
  2. Bowline — a fixed loop that never jams: "the rabbit comes out of the hole, around the tree, back down the hole." Throw-to-a-person loops, fixed docking loops.
  3. Round turn and two half hitches — for mooring rings and anything under load; the load can be adjusted while it holds.
  4. Anchor bend — securing a line permanently to a ring.
  5. Fender hitch — quick, adjustable fender height from a rail or cleat.
  6. Clove hitch — a quick temporary tie to a rail, post or ring — two loops that lock, re-tied in seconds.
  7. Figure-of-eight — a stopper knot in the line's end; it stops a line running out through a block or fairlead.

Practice each until your hands do it without your eyes — that's the standard for the practical induction.

»» KNOWLEDGE CHECKWhich knot for a mooring ring under load where you want to adjust the line length? (Round turn and two half hitches.)
»» KNOWLEDGE CHECKWhat goes in the end of a line to stop it running out through a fairlead? (A figure-of-eight stopper knot.)

Lesson 5.8 — Beaching & beach landings ≈ 7 min

»» CLUB MEDIA SLOTVIDEO - M5-07 beach approach

The club allows beach landings in flat calm conditions on sandy beaches only — and discourages it where an anchoring option exists. Hull and cooling system damage are one inattentive minute away.

  1. Approach at 5 kn ceiling — walking pace for the final stretch, clear approach with no rocks, bow lookout watching depth and swimmers.
  2. Trim the engine up as you get shallow — and remember trimmed up means less steering. Keep the propeller in the water for steerage and cooling until the last moment.
  3. As the bow kisses the sand: neutral, and engine OFF immediately — sand sucked into the cooling system wrecks the water pump.
  4. Passenger steps off the side to hold the bow. Or better: stern-to — turn so the stern faces the beach, use the port boarding ladder, prop pointing away from the ladder, and remember the bait board comes off before the engine tilts.
  5. Leaving: reverse off the beach, then anchor properly. Never leave the boat sitting on the sand.
»» KNOWLEDGE CHECKBow-on the sand, engine still running to "hold her there". What's happening to your water pump? (It's sucking sand — engine off the moment the bow touches, every time.)

Lesson 5.9 — Overnight in sheltered bays ≈ 5 min

Overnighting is allowed under club policy — the SF545 has no cabin, so plan gear accordingly (full-length cover, dry bags, warm kit). The rules that matter:

  • »»Moored or anchored safely before the night cut-off — no operations within 30 minutes after sunset or 60 minutes before sunrise.
  • »»At anchor: 5:1 chain, set and confirmed by transit; anchor light on all night, navigation lights off.
  • »»In a berth: bow, stern and two springs made fast; navigation lights off.
  • »»Securely fastened and safe to access — safe boarding access suited to the tide.
  • »»The boat is checked by operations overnight at each base — leave it as the checklist expects (covers on, electrics off, keys in the lockbox).
»» KNOWLEDGE CHECKSunset 20:40. You want to overnight in a sheltered bay rather than return to the berth. By when must you be anchored and set up? (Anchored with the anchor light on by 21:10 — 30 minutes after sunset — with safe access arranged for the tide.)
