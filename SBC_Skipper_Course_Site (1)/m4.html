Module 4 - Boat handling & marina manoeuvres

SBC Skipper · Module 4 of 8 · ≈ 75 minutes · Quiz: 10 questions served from a 30-question bank, 80% to pass

Boat handling is the part of this course that lives in your hands, not your head. Every skill here is demonstrated at your induction and practised until it's boring — because boring is what competent looks like.

Lesson 4.1 — Controls & helm ≈ 8 min

»» CLUB MEDIA SLOTVIDEO - M1-03/04 refresher

The SF545 is steered by its outboard, powered by its throttle, and protected by its kill cord. Three habits from day one:

  • »»Kill cord clipped to you whenever the engine is running.
  • »»Throttle friction set so the throttle doesn't creep — adjust it once and check it each trip.
  • »»Trim switches work from the throttle and the engine itself (backup) — trim down for steerage and take-off, up for the ride.

Club conditions while underway: everyone seated, limbs inboard, no bow riding ever, no hard, fast, sharp or erratic steering, and reversing only at very slow speed in calm conditions — reversing into chop or at speed swamps a boat this size.

»» KNOWLEDGE CHECKWhich two club steering rules apply at all times? (No sharp/erratic steering; reversing only very slowly in calm conditions.)

Lesson 4.2 — Low-speed control ≈ 8 min

»» CLUB MEDIA SLOTVIDEO - M4-02 throttle bursts + pivot

An outboard steers by pushing water — no flow, no steering. That single fact explains everything about close-quarters handling:

  • »»Throttle in short bursts, neutral between. Each burst pushes water past the rudder and moves the boat; in neutral she glides. This is how you park precisely.
  • »»Momentum is your enemy at low speed. Plan the approach so you arrive nearly stopped — you can always add a burst, but you can't un-momentum.
  • »»Throttle assists turning: a short burst while the helm is over kicks the stern around — the tight-turn technique from your induction sea trial.
  • »»Trim right down for maximum rudder bite at low speed.
  1. In reverse the outboard swings the stern the opposite way to your steering input — expect the stern to walk, and correct with short bursts rather than holding the helm over.
»» KNOWLEDGE CHECKYou're 5 m off the berth, drifting slightly wide, still at walking pace. Fix? (Neutral, a short burst with helm over toward the berth — momentum first, then steer.)

Lesson 4.3 — Departing the berth ≈ 7 min

»» CLUB MEDIA SLOTVIDEO - M4-03 departing berth

Before the engine starts: fenders positioned, lines ready to release in order, crew briefed on their jobs. Then:

  1. Engine on, trim down.
  2. Release lines stern-first logic — which line is holding you against the wind? That one comes off last.
  3. Push off gently; let the boat fall away from the dock.
  4. Out of the pen at idle, then 5 kn until you're clear of the marina structures.

Wind awareness — the three departure problems:

  • »»Crosswind: a crosswind pushes the bow (or stern) onto the dock as you leave — plan the push-off direction so the wind works for you, and never let the bow blow back onto the neighbour's hull.
  • »»Wind pinning you onto the dock: hold the forward spring, power gently ahead — the stern kicks out — then slip the spring and reverse clear.
  • »»Wind blowing you off: hold the stern line, let the bow fall away on the wind, slip when the bow is clear and motor out.
»» KNOWLEDGE CHECKWind is pinning you against the dock as you're ready to leave. Technique? (Push the stern out against the wind, ease away with a burst while the bow swings clear — and fend off the neighbours' hulls.)

Lesson 4.4 — Marina discipline & berth types ≈ 7 min

»» CLUB MEDIA SLOTVIDEO - M4-04 marina transit

Inside a marina, everything is a structure: 5 knots, no exceptions, no wake. Your wake rocks other members' boats, dumps dinghies off docks and earns complaints with your name on them.

Berth types you'll meet: drive-in pens (back out, or reverse in), pole/pile berths (dock between poles — approach into wind/current, lay lines as you come in), and side-tie docks. The technique differs slightly; the principles don't: slow, into the elements, lines ready, fenders set.

Courtesy is part of the rules: give way to boats already transiting the fairway (the main channel between the pens), don't block a pen while you fumble, and keep your voice down near moored homes.

»» KNOWLEDGE CHECKWhy 5 kn inside the marina even when it looks empty? (Everything is a structure within 200 m — the law, plus wake damage and the club's name on your hull.)

Lesson 4.5 — Hold station & pivot ≈ 7 min

»» CLUB MEDIA SLOTVIDEO - M4-05 hold station

Two drills from your induction that make everything else possible:

  • »»Hold station: stop the boat and keep it within a boat-length for 30 seconds in wind. Technique: bow (or stern) to the elements, idle bursts to correct drift, trim down for bite.
  • »»Pivot in your own length: helm hard over, short burst astern or ahead alternating — the boat swings nearly in place. This is how you turn around in a fairway, recover position, and line up a berth.

Both are wind-and-tide reading in disguise: know which way the elements push your bow, and you can park the boat anywhere.

»» KNOWLEDGE CHECKWind 12 kn on your port beam (your left side at right angles to the boat). Where does the boat want to go, and what stops it? (Drifting to starboard — hold with intermittent bursts, bow into the wind for the easiest station-keeping.)

Lesson 4.6 — Berthing: arriving ≈ 10 min

»» CLUB MEDIA SLOTVIDEO - M4-06a berth approach
»» CLUB MEDIA SLOTVIDEO - M4-06b securing

The setup decides the landing. Fenders down at dock height (Module 1), lines coiled and led — bow, stern and two springs for the final make-fast. Crew briefed: which line first, where they step, what they hold.

The approach:

  1. Plan into the wind or tide — whichever pushes you, approach so it slows you, not speeds you.
  2. The legal ceiling inside the zones is 5 kn — but the final approach is walking pace or less, near-idle: 30–45° to the dock, aiming to stop alongside with the bow line point abeam. Arrive nearly stopped (Lesson 4.2).
  3. Last 5 m: neutral, throttle bursts to steer, let her glide in.
  4. Line first to the dock (bow or stern depending on elements), engine to neutral, then the rest.
  5. Secure: bow, stern, two springs. Fenders adjusted after she settles.

Practice across different berths — the induction requires controlled docking in at least two different berths, because every berth has its own wind shadow and current. If an approach goes wrong at 3 m off: abort early, go around, try again. Aborting is seamanship; crunching is not.

»» KNOWLEDGE CHECKWind blowing you onto the dock as you approach. What changes? (A wider, more controlled approach — come in even slower, get the bow line on early before the wind sets you down, and fend off.)
»» KNOWLEDGE CHECKWhich four lines secure the boat for the night? (Bow, stern and two springs.)

Lesson 4.7 — Coming alongside pontoons & wharves ≈ 6 min

»» CLUB MEDIA SLOTVIDEO - M4-07 pontoon alongside

Beach-landing steps, restaurant jetties, Islington Bay wharves — the same skill at any pontoon: into the elements, 5 kn ceiling but walking pace for the final approach, lines ready, crew holding the boat, engine off before anyone steps on the ladder.

  • »»Crew comms: "bow line first" / "hold her there" / "make fast stern" — one voice, the skipper's.
  • »»Passengers wait for instructions before stepping off — never onto a moving dock.
  • »»At a beach ladder pickup (swimmers, beach landings): engine off — the swim ladder sits next to the propeller.
»» KNOWLEDGE CHECKWho steps off first at a pontoon, and when does the engine stop? (Passengers step when told, on the instruction of the skipper — and the engine is off before anyone uses the ladder or steps aboard.)

Lesson 4.8 — Open-water handling & trim ≈ 10 min

»» CLUB MEDIA SLOTVIDEO - M4-08 choppy-day run with trim demo

The deep-vee hull rewards good trim:

  • »»Getting onto the plane: trim right down, power on steadily, bow lifts, then trim up as she settles onto the plane. Passengers seated, everyone holding.
  • »»The ride: trim up for speed and a dry boat in a head chop; trim down when it's rough or the load is light.
  • »»Head seas: reduce speed to keep the bow working, meet them at a slight angle if needed — never let the boat launch and slam.
  • »»Following seas: the dangerous ones. Speed matches the sea — too slow and the sea overtakes and surfs you; too fast and you dig the bow. Keep steering authority, trim down slightly, and if the sea chases you, change course or speed before it decides for you.
  • »»Emergency stop from speed (sea-trial skill): helm straight, throttle back in one decisive motion, let her settle — know how far your boat carries at 20 kn.
  • »»Cornering at speed: reduce first, then turn — passengers and gear positioned for the lean.

Checks during the trip (SOP): fuel level, engine temps and pressures, voltage, passenger comfort, weather changes — and act on plotter alarms from the club's monitoring.

»» KNOWLEDGE CHECKA 1 m following sea behind you at 18 kn. Why is this the dangerous setup? (The sea can overtake and surf the boat, digging the bow — match speed to the sea or change course before it pushes you.)

Lesson 4.9 — Rough-water discipline ≈ 6 min

Conditions deteriorating — act early, in this order:

  1. Lifejackets on and secured (already worn — now check them).
  2. Hatches sealed, gear stowed, centre of gravity low — everyone seated, low and central.
  3. Speed down to maintain control; take seas at the angle that keeps the bow working.
  4. Head for shelter at the first sign of real deterioration — the forecast, the tide and your distance all argue for leaving early, not late.
  5. Crew briefed: what you're doing and what they should hold.
»» KNOWLEDGE CHECKThe forecast was 12 kn; it's now blowing 22 against the tide. What changes? (This is the Enclosed-Water band — reduce speed, head for shelter, and check in with the club; the sea will be steeper than the numbers suggested.)

Lesson 4.10 — Fishing & onboard activities ≈ 5 min

»» CLUB MEDIA SLOTSTILLS - M1-09 bait board
»» CLUB MEDIA SLOTSTILLS - M3-06 towing stills
  • »»Bait board: fit it for fishing; remove it before tilting the engine — the cowling strikes it.
  • »»Rods and tackle: stowed forward on the berths while underway; sinkers down by the reel, never swinging on the rod tip; loose hooks and knives stowed; a bucket of water and rags ready before the lines go in.
  • »»Lines and the propeller: all lines in before the engine goes in gear.
  • »»Filleting onboard is against club policy — use the Kai Ika service at Z Pier.
  • »»Swimming off the boat: engine off, key out, propeller hazard briefed — the ladder is beside the prop.
  • »»Towing: ski pole only for release-able tows (skier/wakeboarder); direct-towed toys via a bridle (a V-shaped rope pair) on both rear cleats; observer 10+ aboard; towed person in a lifejacket; no towing after dark.
»» KNOWLEDGE CHECKYou're tilted up at anchor for lunch and want to run home. Checklist? (Bait board off, trim down, all lines and rods stowed, swimmers clear, lifejackets on before the engine starts.)
