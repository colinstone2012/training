Module 0 - Welcome & common terms

SBC Skipper · Module 0 of 8 · ≈ 25 minutes · Acknowledgement (no quiz)

»» CLUB MEDIA SLOTVIDEO - M0-01 welcome from the club

Welcome aboard. Your SBC membership is a fleet of twenty-four identical Haines Hunter SF545s across three Auckland marinas — Westhaven (Z Pier), Gulf Harbour (Fairway Bay) and Half Moon Bay — ready when you are, no maintenance, no towing, no storage. Book it in the app, check in, cast off.

How a booking works: reserve in the SBC app (weeks ahead or last-minute) → arrive → check in with the hull-condition photos → get your lockbox code → keys → go. Return by the end of your slot, refuelled, hosed down and clean. Full walk-through in your practical induction and in Appendix A of the course library.

This module also grounds the words. Boating has its own language, and the rest of the course uses it without stopping to translate — so we do that translating here, once, properly. Learn these and every module that follows reads easier.

Lesson 0.1 — Welcome & how the club works ≈ 6 min

»» CLUB MEDIA SLOTVIDEO - M0-01 welcome from the club

Membership, not ownership. No mooring fees, no winter haul-out, no resale headache. Show up, push off, go fishing — the keys are in the lockbox, the boat's checked over and full of fuel.

  • »»24 vessels. Every boat is the same boat: Haines Hunter SF545, one spec, learn one and you know all twenty-four.
  • »»3 marinas. Westhaven (Z Pier), Gulf Harbour (Fairway Bay), Half Moon Bay — pick whichever suits the day.
  • »»20+ islands, 4,000 km² of marine park. Waiheke, Rotoroa, Kawau — the Gulf is your playground.
  • »»The crew answers the phone, runs the training, and checks the boats. You're not a customer here — you're a member.
»» KNOWLEDGE CHECKYou want to take a boat out Saturday morning. What do you do first? (Book it in the SBC app — then arrive, check in with the hull photos, collect the keys.)
»» KNOWLEDGE CHECKWhose job is it to check the boat over before your slot? (The crew's — the boat arrives checked over and full of fuel. You confirm with the check-in photos.)

Lesson 0.2 — Common terms: the boat ≈ 10 min

The words we use for the boat itself — the same words in every lesson, every briefing, and at the dock.

  • »»Port / starboard — left and right, looking forward. Port = left, starboard = right. (The red sidelight is the port side — red-left coming in.)
  • »»Bow / stern — the front and the back of the boat.
  • »»Hull — the body of the boat. The SF545's is a deep-vee hull — it cuts chop instead of slapping over it.
  • »»Gunwale ("gunnel") — the top edge of the boat's side.
  • »»Transom — the flat back of the boat, where the outboard mounts.
  • »»Cleat — the metal fitting you tie lines to.
  • »»Fender — the soft bumper hung between the boat and the dock.
  • »»Spring lines — the diagonal lines that stop the boat sliding forward or back alongside the dock.
  • »»Scuppers — the drain holes along the deck edge (that's how the self-draining cockpit drains).
  • »»Bimini — the folding shade canopy.
  • »»Cowling — the engine's cover (remove the bait board before tilting — the cowling strikes it).
  • »»Tell-tale — the little stream of cooling water out the side of a running engine. No tell-tale = no cooling = shut down.
  • »»Plotter — the chart screen at the helm; the boat's digital map and navigator.
  • »»Windlass — the powered anchor winch on the bow.
  • »»Berth — your marina parking spot (the pen you dock in).
  • »»On the plane — skimming on top of the water, the fast smooth part of the ride.
»» KNOWLEDGE CHECKA crew member says "throw the line to the cleat on the starboard side." Which side, and what are they tying to? (The right side, looking forward — and the metal fitting made for tying lines to.)
»» KNOWLEDGE CHECKWater is pooling in the cockpit. What's blocked? (The scuppers — the drain holes along the deck edge.)

Lesson 0.3 — Common terms: on the water ≈ 8 min

The words for what the boat is doing, and the words you'll hear on the radio.

  • »»Underway — not anchored, not made fast to shore. (An engine ticking in neutral still counts.)
  • »»Making way — moving through the water. Underway but stopped (drifting, no way through the water) is a different state with different rules.
  • »»Give way — let the other boat go first: slow down and pass behind.
  • »»Stand on — hold your course and speed; the boat that keeps going while the other gives way.
  • »»Knots (kn) — speed at sea. 10 kn ≈ 18.5 km/h. The Gulf speeds are all in knots.
  • »»No-wake zone — keep it slow, no wash.
  • »»Anchorage — a sheltered spot to stay a while.
  • »»Transit — two fixed marks lined up with part of your boat; it keeps you on the safe path — and tells you whether your anchor is holding.
  • »»Chart / plotter — the map of the water. Chart 532 is the paper backup in the grab bag; the plotter is its screen.
  • »»Chart datum — the depth baseline: charted depths are measured from roughly the lowest tide, not today's water.
  • »»VHF / Ch 16 — the always-on radio. Ch 16 is the international distress, safety and calling channel.
  • »»EPIRB — the beacon that broadcasts your position when life is in danger.
  • »»Mayday / Pan-Pan — Mayday = grave and imminent danger; Pan-Pan = urgent, but no one in danger yet.
  • »»MOB — man overboard. The drill you'll learn in Module 7.
  • »»Line — a rope, once it comes aboard. Rope lives ashore; lines work the boat.
  • »»Bitter end — the end of a line that gets made fast. ("Make sure the bitter end of the anchor rode is tied on.")
  • »»Seacock — the valve on a through-hull fitting; it opens and closes the hole in the hull. Know where yours are.
  • »»Steerage way — the minimum speed at which the rudder actually works. Below steerage way, you're drifting, not steering.
»» KNOWLEDGE CHECKThe radio says a boat is "underway but making no way." What is it doing? (Not anchored or tied up — but stopped in the water, drifting.)
»» KNOWLEDGE CHECKForecast says 15 kn. How fast is that in car terms? (About 28 km/h — knots are the sea's speed unit.)
