Module 6 - Safety equipment & communications

SBC Skipper · Module 6 of 8 · ≈ 60 minutes · Quiz: 12 questions served from a 36-question bank, 80% to pass

When something goes wrong out there, you get help in exactly three ways: you talk (VHF), you call (phone), or you broadcast without talking (EPIRB and flares). This module teaches all three — plus where every piece of safety gear lives on your boat and how each one works. Gear you can find in 60 seconds is gear that works at 3 seconds' notice.

Lesson 6.1 — Lifejackets, operationally ≈ 8 min

»» CLUB MEDIA SLOTVIDEO - M2-01 refresher

Module 2 taught the rule; this is the operational layer:

  • »»Inventory: 6 × 175 N manually-inflating adult jackets + 1 × child foam jacket (22–40 kg), stowed under the port passenger seat. That's inventory, not capacity — the boat carries 6 persons including you per the survey.
  • »»More than one child? Bring extra child jackets — one on board is one.
  • »»Own jackets welcome — for every person, correctly sized.
  • »»Serviceability: cylinder seated and screwed home, firing head intact (green indicator if fitted), no tears, straps not sun-rotted, back-up whistle attached. A jacket that hasn't been looked at in two seasons is a rumour of a lifejacket.
  • »»Never fire the manual inflate on a serviceable jacket — it spends the CO2 cylinder and takes the jacket out of service until rearmed. Demonstrations use a training jacket at induction.

The connection to everything else in this module: a person in the water wearing a lifejacket is findable — by eye, by flare light, by the search pattern. Wear = findable.

»» KNOWLEDGE CHECKYour daughter's friend (28 kg) is coming, and the boat's child jacket is already claimed. What must happen? (An extra correctly sized child jacket comes from home — no exceptions.)

Lesson 6.2 — VHF: the always-on lifeline ≈ 12 min

»» CLUB MEDIA SLOTVIDEO - M6-01 VHF controls + squelch
»» CLUB MEDIA SLOTVIDEO - M6-02 scripted Mayday

Why VHF beats your phone: it broadcasts to everyone listening (a net, not a thread), it runs off the boat's batteries — and on these boats it's wired directly to the batteries, always powered: turn the volume dial up and it's live. No app, no coverage needed.

Setting it up: volume up, then squelch just above static — high enough to kill the hiss, low enough not to filter out real calls. Monitor Channel 16 throughout the trip — that's the international distress, safety and calling channel. A MAYDAY sticker and the SBC emergency numbers sit beside the radio on every hull.

Radio checks happen at pre-departure (volume, squelch, channel set) — not on Channel 16. Use a working channel or the Coastguard app's check feature.

Only members holding a Radio Operator Certificate may use VHF for trip reports. Anyone may use VHF in an emergency — a Mayday from an unlicensed skipper in a real emergency is always better than silence.

Spoken clearly, standard format (this is the phonetic alphabet's home ground — Module 3):

  • »»MAYDAY — grave and imminent danger (flooding, swamping, grounding, fire, person unconscious in the water):

1. "Mayday, Mayday, Mayday" 2. "This is [vessel name], [callsign ×3]" — e.g. "This is Taika, Zulu Mike Echo One Two Three, Zulu Mike Echo One Two Three, Zulu Mike Echo One Two Three" — the vessel name goes with the callsign 3. "Mayday [vessel name and callsign]" 4. "Position ..." (plotter position or transit description) 5. "Nature of emergency ... assistance required ... people on board ... sea conditions ... Over" Then release the key and listen. If no reply — repeat, and activate the EPIRB.

Memory aid — MIPDANIO: Mayday — Identity (this is, with MMSI) — Position (as accurate as possible) — Distress detail (what's wrong) — Assistance required — Numbers on board — Information — Over. Our script above already runs in this order.

DSC in practice (whether your hull's set has DSC active is confirmed at induction): the red distress button under its cover does two things. Press and hold for 5 seconds — a generic distress alert goes out digitally on Ch 70 with your MMSI and (if the GPS is connected) your position, then follow with the voice Mayday above. Press and release, then choose from the list — fire, flooding, grounding, MOB and more — then hold 5 seconds: the alert tells rescuers what help you need before you've said a word. An accidental press: DSC distresses can be cancelled — the procedure is on the sticker beside the radio; don't just switch the set off.

  • »»PAN-PAN — urgent, but no immediate danger (loss of steering, drift, engine failure near traffic): "Pan-Pan, Pan-Pan, Pan-Pan — All stations, all stations — this is [callsign] — position — problem — assistance required — people on board — Over."
  • »»SÉCURITÉ — a safety message for other vessels (e.g. a hazard you've found drifting).

If the radio set has DSC (digital selective calling — a red distress button under a cover), pressing and holding it transmits your position and MMSI digitally to all stations and ashore. Whether the club's sets have DSC active is confirmed at induction — the sticker beside the radio tells you the procedure that applies to your hull.

»» KNOWLEDGE CHECKOrder a Mayday: location → nature → people → conditions → "Over"? Build it: Mayday ×3 → callsign ×3 → position → what's wrong → how many aboard → seas → Over.
»» KNOWLEDGE CHECKYour engine dies 2 nm from the moorings with no danger in sight. Mayday or Pan-Pan? (Pan-Pan — urgent assistance, no immediate danger to life.)

Lesson 6.3 — Trip reports & two independent ways to call ≈ 6 min

Module 2 covered the plan; here's the comms logic:

  • »»Trip Report before departure — Coastguard app for everyone; VHF only if you hold a Radio Operator Certificate. Contents: people on board, brief plan, ETA, listening channel (Ch 16).
  • »»Close it within 30 minutes of returning — an unclosed report starts an overdue procedure.
  • »»Two independent ways to communicate, always (regional bylaws require it — and it's how you stay findable when one system fails): VHF (battery-wired, broadcast) + mobile phone (*500 for Coastguard assistance, 111 for emergencies). The plotter's connection to the club is your third layer: if you go quiet, they already know where to look.
»» KNOWLEDGE CHECKPhone's dead, VHF's fine — you still have two independent means. True or false? (True — VHF + the plotter link to the club; but a waterproof phone in a grab bag is the standard third layer. Check what you actually have before casting off.)

Lesson 6.4 — EPIRB: the broadcast that speaks for you ≈ 6 min

»» CLUB MEDIA SLOTVIDEO - M6-03 EPIRB locate/show

The EPIRB lives on the port-side wall in front of the passenger seating. It's a 406 MHz beacon, registered to the club — so if it ever activates, the response is built around the fleet's details. Know which hull's beacon you're carrying.

When it activates: grave and imminent danger only — the abandon-ship scenario, or a situation where you cannot send a call and life is at risk. It transmits your identity and position to the Rescue Coordination Centre New Zealand via satellite.

The difference that matters:

  • »»VHF Mayday = a conversation. You tell them what's wrong; they answer, coordinate, arrive.
  • »»EPIRB = a silent alarm with your position. No conversation, slower response chain — but it keeps transmitting for days, from a lifejacket pocket, a liferaft, or the water.

In the abandon-ship sequence it comes after the Mayday call: don lifejackets → Mayday with position → call the club → grab bag, torch, EPIRB → activate the EPIRB → fuel off → electrics isolated → off the boat in order → huddle. (Full sequence in Module 7.)

»» KNOWLEDGE CHECKVHF dead, boat sinking fast, everyone in lifejackets. What fires first? (The EPIRB — grave and imminent danger is exactly its mission; it broadcasts your position while you deal with the emergency.)
»» KNOWLEDGE CHECKYou've lost steering 5 nm out and are anchored safely, awaiting a tow. EPIRB? (No — Pan-Pan by VHF or *500 is the right channel. EPIRB is for grave and imminent danger, not inconvenience.)

Lesson 6.5 — Flares, smoke & the grab bag ≈ 10 min

»» CLUB MEDIA SLOTVIDEO - M6-04 grab bag laid out
»» CLUB MEDIA SLOTVIDEO - M6-05 flare demonstration

The grab bag lives under the passenger seating. Contents:

  • »»First aid kit (with manual, notebook and pen)
  • »»2 × orange smoke floats and 2 × handheld flares (day and night signalling)
  • »»Emergency knife · binoculars · Chart 532 and charting equipment
  • »»Anchor winch handle (the emergency manual release) · toolkit · ship's horn · spanner
  • »»Skipper's checklist · VHF Users Handbook · Collision Regulations handbook

Using them properly:

  • »»Red hand-held flare: hold arm's length, downwind of the boat, angled high and away from people, fuel and the canopy. Strike, and hold it burning downwind and over the side — burning dregs drip. Burns on a rescue boat are a second emergency.
  • »»Orange smoke float: activate and drop it in the water to leeward (downwind) — smoke sits downwind of the boat and that's where searchers look. Day-time signal: smoke reads to aircraft at distance.
  • »»Red flares read best at night; smoke reads best in daylight. Carry both because you don't get to choose the hour.
  • »»Check the expiry dates — the club tracks them on the safety equipment list, but look at the date if you ever use the bag. Expired or used items: report them in-app so they're replaced before the next trip.
  • »»Never fire flares at people, boats or fuel — and never as a joke. False distress signals are a crime.
»» KNOWLEDGE CHECKIt's a bright afternoon, vessel disabled and drifting. Which signals work best? (Orange smoke — daylight signal — plus VHF Pan-Pan; save the red flares for night or aircraft response.)
»» KNOWLEDGE CHECKWind is blowing from the shore toward you. Where does the smoke float go? (Leeward — the downwind side, so the smoke drifts away from the boat and over the water where it's visible.)

Lesson 6.6 — Fire, first aid & the extinguisher ≈ 8 min

The extinguisher: dry powder, starboard side wall in front of the helm. Dry powder suits fuel and electrical fires — the two fire types this boat actually gets.

Engine fire — the sequence:

  1. Stop the outboard.
  2. Shut the fuel supply (remote fuel shut-off — know its location from induction).
  3. Isolate the batteries (kill the electrics that feed sparks).
  4. Discharge the dry powder into the cowling air intake — never remove the cowling; that feeds air to the fire.
  5. Keep everyone clear of smoke; discharge the extinguisher fully.

If the fire won't die: Mayday on Ch 16, *500, and call the club — and prepare for the worst-case while you still have choices. Shout "FIRE! FIRE! FIRE!" the moment smoke or heat is found — passengers move away from smoke and the skipper takes control of the situation early.

First aid kit: manual, notebook and pen inside. Treat what you're trained to treat; use the manual; note what you used — report used items in-app so the kit is restocked for the next crew. For anything serious: Ch 16 (Coastguard can route medical advice) or 111 with your position.

»» KNOWLEDGE CHECKSmoke from the engine cowling. Order the actions? (Shout FIRE → stop engine → fuel shut-off → batteries isolated → powder into the air intake → Mayday if it persists.)
»» KNOWLEDGE CHECKThe extinguisher gauge is in the red or the seal is broken. Go or no-go? (No-go — report it; a fire extinguisher that won't fire is ballast.)

Lesson 6.7 — Coastguard: your assistance network ≈ 5 min

Your SBC membership includes Coastguard membership — the network that answers when things go sideways:

  • »»Emergency (life in danger): VHF Ch 16 Mayday, or 111 and ask for Police (they run search and rescue).
  • »»Non-urgent assistance (broken down, out of fuel, need a tow): *500 from a mobile or 0800 BOATIE (0800 262 843), select 1.
  • »»Medical advice at sea: Ch 16 to Coastguard — they can connect a doctor and plan a meeting point.
  • »»Trip reports through the Coastguard app tie all of this together (Module 2).

And remember the club layer: in-app messaging reaches the SBC team, and the plotter keeps operations aware of your position and health. Use both — there is no prize for struggling alone.

»» KNOWLEDGE CHECKEngine dead, anchored safely, 3 nm from the marina, no danger. Who do you call and on what? (Coastguard non-urgent: *500 or 0800 BOATIE — a tow can be arranged; keep Ch 16 in the background and update the club in-app.)
